## Design Specificity Verdict

**The logic is this product's; the page is a template.**

Authored and specific: the two-phase dish model (`startTime → heatOffTime` on the heat, then `restSeconds`), the per-food option axis (rice has one state worth naming, steak has three), signed serve offsets so a starter lands early, and the runsheet anchoring to `mealTime` rather than the last finish so a late dish does not drag the meal earlier (`static/js/core/runsheet.js:29-31`). The copy carries that model faithfully — "Off the heat at 25:00, then rest 10 min" appears only for foods that rest. No generic SaaS app produces those sentences. The Fraunces/Manrope pairing and the warm peach-and-green backdrop are a deliberate cookbook register, not Inter-on-white.

Category-interchangeable: the composition. Two translucent rounded cards in `repeat(auto-fit, minmax(280px,1fr))`, a slogan chip top-right, form left and output right. Swap the strings and this is an invoicing tool.

The decisive gap: **this product exists to place dishes against a clock, and there is no representation of time anywhere in the visuals.** A four-dish plan with 25/20/8/10-minute cooks and staggered starts renders as four identically sized rectangles of `label: value` prose (`static/js/planning.js:346-365`). No bar, no rail, no relative width, no "you are here". The `::before` gradient stripe on `.schedule-item` is the only gesture toward a timeline, and it is the same 3px on every item regardless of duration.

**Deterministic scan.** CLI detector over `index.html` and `timer.html`: **0 findings, exit 0** — and that result is uninformative here, not reassuring. The discriminating classes arrive at runtime via Alpine (`:class="alertType"` at `timer.html:93`), so the static-HTML engine has nothing to match. The in-page detector, injected live, found **6 anti-patterns per page**:

- **index.html** — `low-contrast` ×3 on white-on-accent button labels: `#add-food-btn` 2.8:1 on `#ff6b4a`, `#custom-food-add` 2.6:1 on `#2bb673`, `#start-timer-btn` 2.6:1 (all 16–17px/600, so 4.5:1 required); `nested-cards` ×2 (`.food-item` and `.schedule-item` inside `.panel`); `text-occlusion` — `span.serve-offset-unit` "min" **100% covered** by `section.schedule-display.panel`.
- **timer.html** — `low-contrast` on Pause 2.0:1 on `#f4a933`, Reset 3.9:1 on `#e5484d`, `+ Add Food` 2.8:1; `gpt-thin-border-wide-shadow` ×2; `nested-cards` ×2.

False positives: the two `nested-cards` pairs (rows inside a panel is the intended, legible structure) and `gpt-thin-border-wide-shadow` (that *is* this project's established `--shadow-soft` language). The `text-occlusion` finding is the P0 below, seen from another angle. The button-label contrast findings are all true positives.

**The earlier `side-tab` hook finding, revisited.** I previously classified `.alert-popup.food-start` / `.alert-popup.all-done` (4px amber/green left border) as intentional and load-bearing. That holds — it is the only channel distinguishing "start a dish" from "all done", and removing it deletes the signal. But the deeper reading is better: **the state is encoded in hue alone**, on 4px, with no icon, no text label and no shape difference. Glanced at across a kitchen, or by a red-green colour-blind cook, that is the actual failure. The fix is a redundant cue, not a thinner border.

**Visual overlays:** injection succeeded on both pages (12 overlay elements rendered per page), but the live server has since been stopped, so no overlay is currently visible in your browser.

## Overall Impression

A genuinely well-modelled product wearing a generic layout, with four defects that cost it far more than their size. The core is sound: the schedule maths, the two-phase dish, the persistence, the clipboard runsheet. Then a cook cannot delete a dish, the page never tells them what time to put the chicken on despite computing exactly that, and the page their primary button leads to renders its screen-reader text on screen. The single biggest opportunity is not polish — it is that **the schedule is the product and it is rendered as prose.** Give time a shape and most of the hierarchy, trust and "what do I do first" problems dissolve at once.

## What's Working

1. **The two-phase dish model and the copy that carries it.** Rest is declared per food, not per option (`static/js/core/foods.js:60-65`), so "Off the heat at 25:00, then rest 10 min" appears for chicken and steak and never for carrots, and the runsheet emits a separate indented action line for it. It eliminates a whole class of real error — pulling a steak at "done" instead of resting it — without asking the user to know the concept exists.

2. **Progressive disclosure plus write-on-every-change persistence.** `writePlan` fires on every mutation (`static/js/planning.js:322`), not on submit; a mid-flow reload restores both rows and the rendered schedule. Combined with disclosure that only shows controls that currently mean something (the ⟲ reset, the offset styling, the whole schedule panel), the page never presents a control that does nothing. A kitchen is an interruption machine; surviving a reload is what separates a tool from a toy.

3. **The clipboard runsheet.** Numbered, clock-anchored, with indented sub-actions and correct `mealTime` anchoring. It is the only part of this product designed for the actual scene — hands busy, screen out of reach.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] `.food-item` overflows its panel; the remove ✕ is unclickable at every desktop width

`grid-template-columns: minmax(150px, 1fr) minmax(120px, 0.6fr) auto auto auto` has a fixed min-content width of ~611px, but the two-column `.layout` gives `.food-selector` a ~500px box. Measured spill past the panel: **+152px at 1400, +146px at 1280, +202px at 980, +279px at 800**. `.container` is capped at 1120px, so widening the window never helps; only the `max-width: 700px` single-column collapse fixes it. No page scrollbar ever appears to hint at it.

Three independent methods agree the button is dead: `elementFromPoint` at its centre returns `.schedule-item` / its `<strong>`; a real Playwright `click()` **times out after 3000ms** at 1400/1280/980/800; the row count does not change. At 480px it works (the row wraps).

**Why it matters:** the only delete on the page is gone. A cook who picks the wrong dish has no way to remove it except resizing the window or clearing localStorage. It is still tab-focusable, so a keyboard user reaches and can fire a button they cannot see — deleting a row with no visible confirmation.

**Fix:** give `.food-item` two rows on a fluid grid — `grid-template-columns: minmax(0,1fr) minmax(0,0.7fr) auto` with `grid-template-areas: "food option remove" / "time serve remove"` — and `min-width: 0` on the selects so they can actually shrink. Add `min-width: 0` to `.panel` so this class of bug cannot silently paint outside a card again.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P1] `.visually-hidden` has no CSS rule, so screen-reader text renders on screen

Verified: the class appears **3 times in `timer.html` and zero times in the stylesheet**. The span at `timer.html:50` is fully rendered — `position: static`, `clip: auto`. So the hero countdown reads "**10:00** 10:00 remaining" and "**0:00** 0:00 elapsed", and the `aria-live` region at `timer.html:18` will paint its announcements at the top of the page. This is visible in the screenshots from the current build. Compounding it, `.time-elapsed { color: var(--color-muted) }` overrides `.countdown`'s white, so "ELAPSED" and its value sit grey-on-amber at 1.4–2.7:1 depending on flash phase.

**Why it matters:** this is the destination of the planning page's primary CTA, seen at the moment of maximum stakes, and it reads as a broken build — exactly when the cook is deciding whether to trust the app with dinner. It also means the class does no hiding, so the live region is visually redundant with the countdown.

**Fix:** add the standard clip-rect utility to `styles.css`, and scope `.time-elapsed`'s muted colour so it cannot leak onto the gradient panel.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The schedule never shows clock times; "Serve at" is a screen no-op, and Print discards it

`displaySchedule` always renders `formatTime(item.startTime)` (`static/js/planning.js:351`). The `serve-at` change listener calls `updateSchedule()`, which re-renders byte-identical relative text. `clockTimes()` exists at `static/js/core/runsheet.js:39` and is reached only by `runsheetText()` — i.e. only by Copy. The `@media print` block hides `.runsheet-actions` and prints `#schedule-output`, so the printed sheet reads "Start at: 0:00 / Start at: 15:00" and the serve time the cook just typed is silently thrown away. Separately, `formatTime` is `M:SS` with no hour component (verified in `static/js/core/format.js`), so a 75-minute plan reads "75:00" and "the meal is at 10:00" is indistinguishable from ten o'clock.

**Why it matters:** the app's answer to the only question that matters — *what time do I put the chicken on?* — is computed and then withheld from both the screen and the printout.

**Fix:** when `serveAtMinutes()` is non-null, render clock times as the primary line in `.schedule-item` with the relative offset secondary; route Print through `runsheetText()` so Copy and Print emit the same artifact; teach `formatTime` an hours component.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The two numeric fields have no visible label

`.time-override-input` and `.serve-offset-input` carry aria-labels only (`static/js/planning.js:157`, `:188-191`) — no `<label>`, no `title`, no column header, both suffixed with an identical `min` span. A row reads `[Chicken][Cooked t][25] min [0] min [✕]`. Nothing on the page says the second number is a serve offset, that negative means earlier, or that the first overrides a built-in estimate. At 480px they stack as two indistinguishable "min" boxes.

**Why it matters:** a sighted first-timer cannot learn what the second field is from anywhere in the product, and it is the field most likely to be nudged by a stray scroll wheel.

**Fix:** add a persistent header row above `#food-list` — `Dish · How you want it · Cook time · Serve` — and change the offset's unit from `min` to a signed hint like `min vs. meal` with placeholder `±0`. One `<details>` sentence beneath the list explaining both fields closes the help gap at the same time.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Hardcoded accent colours never remap in dark mode; the focus ring fails non-text contrast

Four hexes are declared once and never re-mapped in the dark block, which only re-declares custom properties. Measured against real painted pixels:

| Selector | Dark | Light | Need |
|---|---|---|---|
| `.total-time` (`#9d2e1a`) | **2.00:1** | pass | 4.5 |
| `.total-time-display strong` (`#9d2e1a`) | **2.36:1** | 6.79 | 4.5 |
| `.chip-live` (`#1b6b46`) | **2.02:1** | 4.75 | 4.5 |
| `.cooking-time` (`#b86010`) | **3.11:1** | **4.08:1** | 4.5 |

`.total-time` is the single most important number on the planning panel and the least legible thing on the page. `.chip-live` is the timer's *status* indicator. `.cooking-time` fails in **light mode too**, so it is not purely a theming bug. Separately the focus ring `outline: 3px solid rgba(255, 107, 74, 0.35)` composites to **1.44:1 on white panels / 1.79:1 on dark** — below WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11's 3:1 for non-text contrast. And the white-on-accent button labels measure 2.0–3.9:1 across both pages.

**Why it matters:** the dark-mode select fix just landed for exactly this root cause. These are the instances it did not reach.

**Fix:** move `#9d2e1a`, `#1b6b46`, `#b86010`, `#1c7a51`, `#13643f` into custom properties with dark values; darken the accent button fills or the label treatment to clear 4.5:1; raise the focus ring to full-opacity `--color-primary-strong` with a contrasting offset.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

## Questions to Consider

1. The schedule is the product and it is rendered as prose. What if `.schedule-item` were a proportional bar on a shared time axis — length = `cookDuration`, hatched tail for `restSeconds`, every bar aligned to one "meal" line? Would you still need "(15 min after previous)", or does that string exist only to compensate for the missing picture?
2. The app computes wall-clock times and shows them only inside a clipboard string. What if "Serve at" were the *first* thing on the page rather than a footnote in the output panel — the anchor the whole plan hangs from, defaulting to "in 90 minutes" — and every time on screen were a real clock time from then on? Does "t=0" ever need to surface in the UI at all?
3. A cook can leave a running timer and return to a planning page that shows no sign of it. What if the header chip were the timer's live status chip, and the CTA read "Return to your cook — 22 min in" whenever a session is live? That slot currently holds "Plan. Sync. Serve."
4. The two numeric fields are the least understood controls on the page, and both exist to admit the built-in times are guesses. What if cook time were framed as an adjustment to a stated estimate ("Chicken · about 25 min · adjust") and the serve offset were a choice ("with the meal / before / after") rather than a signed integer? Would either still need a label?
5. `CLAUDE.md` says kitchen capacity (G13/G14) is open on purpose. The schedule already knows every dish's `[startTime, heatOffTime)` window. What is the cheapest honest thing the planning page could say — not a hob allocator, just "3 dishes are on the heat at 22:00" — and would that one sentence do more for trust than any polish above?
6. Every schedule item has identical visual weight. If the page could emphasise exactly one thing, should it be the total time (currently the loudest element) or the sentence "Start the chicken at 18:55" — the only instruction the cook can act on right now?
